# RF Atlas 1.2 discovery validation

1. **Version and commits.** Version 1.2.0 continues validated 1.1 commit `da87950`. Implementation commit: `bd878c7`. The subsequent release-validation commit contains this report and corrects RDS searched-window accounting without changing decoding decisions. All commits are local; nothing was pushed. Original code remains MIT licensed.

2. **Live scanner performance.** Actual Windows RTL-SDR Blog V4L acquisitions preceded substantial source changes. Manual gain remained 15.7 dB, integration 1 second, acquisition duration 3 seconds, bias tee off. Existing drivers, USB configuration and DLLs were preserved. One receiver owner was enforced. Detailed frequencies, raw spectra, IQ, logs, timestamps and machine-readable reports remain private. Counts below describe candidate channels and observations, not physical transmitters.

3. **Before/after throughput and scan changes.** The matched coarse valid first-pass work covered 9.15 MHz in 29.98 seconds before and 30.13 seconds after: approximately 0.305 versus 0.304 MHz/s. There is no demonstrated acquisition speed gain. Including follow-ups, captures and failures, gross valid coverage per total wall time changed from 0.2645 to 0.2124 MHz/s. The finer comparison changed from 0.1953 to 0.1790 MHz/s. More characterization costs receiver time.

   Controlled replay of identical retained spectra separates detector changes from reception variability: 18 valid coarse spectrum rows produced 0 old narrow detections versus 6 new broad candidates; 27 finer rows produced 42 versus 60. Detector processing took roughly 2.4 versus 4.0 ms and 15.4 versus 22.8 ms respectively. These small one-run timings are indicative, not precision benchmarks.

   The original narrow detector remains unchanged. Survey detection adds separately labelled broad prominence candidates with at least 9 dB contrast and at least 50 kHz width. Their noise basis is the prominence trough rather than the local median. Default amateur, aviation, marine, land-mobile and satellite organizational profiles now request 5 kHz coarse bins and 1 kHz follow-up bins. Existing editable profiles are preserved. The 2 MHz segmentation, 20% tool crop, two requested-bin boundary padding, strict contiguous coverage checks and conservative edge/DC treatment remain. Typical acquisition wall time was about 5 seconds for 3 seconds of requested observation: startup/retuning leaves approximately 2 seconds of overhead. Weak signals, narrow signals in coarse windows and bursts between visits can still be missed. No unsupported gain setting or unmeasured settling-time optimization was enabled.

4. **Discoveries, queue and reports.** Smart Survey visited all seven configured windows, including invalid ones, and measured 45.45 MHz gross across revisits, 9.15 MHz unique. It completed 15 investigations, leaving 3 pending; average enqueue-to-completion latency was 56.50 seconds. Twelve channels appeared in multiple survey runs. Sixty of the 63 channels remained protocol UNKNOWN. This isolated campaign began with an empty catalog, so previously cataloged channels revisited at campaign start numbered zero.

   Quick Survey was also exercised through the actual application controller against the existing catalog for 66.40 seconds: six valid acquisitions, 44 observations involving 20 channels, 17 new catalog records and three previously known channels, four completed investigations, three controller cycles, and 188,416 bytes of logical database growth. Original runtime profile/settings files were restored. Validation-only outstanding jobs were cancelled after receiver shutdown. A discovered stop-path issue was repaired: terminated children now requeue interrupted work under the scheduler lock rather than leaving ghost running statuses.

   `discovery-report --since <explicit-UTC> --until <explicit-UTC> --output <new-private-file.json>` reports visits, actual settings, gross/unique valid bandwidth, failures, observations, distinct candidates, previously known revisits, current UNKNOWN state, verified results/retained identifiers, queue latency/backlog and logical database size. Ordinary segmented surveys are included as well as inventory visits. Instrumented campaign reports add measured host health and initial database size; unavailable health/growth values are null rather than invented. The Smart campaign sampled host CPU at 31.2% mean / 53.0% maximum and host RAM used at approximately 10.51 GB mean / 10.63 GB maximum. These are whole-host measurements with concurrent development work, not scanner-only CPU or process RSS. Its final logical database size was 1,183,744 bytes; no initial size was sampled for that run, so its growth is not asserted.

5. **Fingerprints.** Each new spectral observation can retain resolution, gain, center, bandwidth, SNR, 32-point normalized shape, entropy, flatness, concentration and floor variation. IQ investigations additionally retain bounded envelope variation, relative carrier offset, instantaneous frequency spread, existing energy-based activity/duty estimates, burst durations and inter-event intervals. CFO is relative to tuning, not calibrated oscillator error. Integrated FFTs cannot establish burst durations; finite energy intervals do not establish long-term periodicity.

   Historical matching requires compatible version, source, gain, resolution within 5%, bandwidth within 30%, close centers, SNR within 6 dB and shape RMS error at most 0.12. The score is not a probability. At least two other matching survey runs are required for LIKELY_RECURRING_PATTERN. Frequency spread/std describe those matching measurements with their resolution limits. Smart results: five likely recurring patterns, five channels with similar characteristics, 53 without a comparable match. Identity remains UNPROVEN for every fingerprint.

   Synthetic controls matched 500/500 perturbed examples of the same characteristics, falsely matched 0/500 independent random-shape negatives, and collided on 500/500 indistinguishable examples representing different emitters. Random negatives are easy controls, not a field false-match estimate. These collisions demonstrate why fingerprints cannot prove transmitter identity. Supported verified protocol/device identifiers remain separate evidence; spectral matches never merge device/station records.

6. **Entropy and noise validation.** [rtl-entropy](https://github.com/pwarren/rtl-entropy) is a GPL-3.0 entropy-generator reference, not a mandatory classifier dependency. No source was reused; no cryptographic entropy generation or OS randomness changes were added. Original NumPy/SciPy measurements use normalized PSD entropy, geometric/arithmetic flatness, top-1% concentration and the 40th-minus-10th percentile floor variation. Zero-power inputs report unavailable statistics, and non-finite/negative powers are rejected.

7. **CNN and feature experiments.** [cnn-rtlsdr](https://github.com/randaller/cnn-rtlsdr) explores IQ images and transformed representations but recommends an obsolete TensorFlow/CUDA environment. Its repository license endpoint returned HTTP 404 during review; source/weight reuse permission was not established. No code, data, weights, obsolete environment or DLL instructions were reused.

   The original optional experiment compares the unchanged DSP and existing NumPy MLP with an expanded-feature MLP and a trainable NumPy 1D CNN. The CNN uses five 64-bin views: I, Q, FFT, envelope and instantaneous phase; twelve width-5 kernels, four pooling regions and a softmax head. Expanded features add the new spectral statistics and relative frequency features. Training used 180 development cases; validation and holdout each used 180 separately generated cases. Fresh seed namespaces were 12,110,000 / 12,220,000 / 12,330,000, separate from frozen historical corpora. No evaluation data trained normalization or weights. Rejection thresholds were fixed at .55 score / .12 margin, with existing detection/rail gates. 8. **RDS and NRSC-5.** The [gyaresu SDR examples](https://github.com/gyaresu/sdr) catalog gr-rds and NRSC-5 tools around a historical GNU Radio Live environment. That environment was not installed. [Redsea](https://github.com/windytan/redsea) was reviewed as an RDS reference: its repository license is MIT and individual synchronization source carries ISC-style permission. Nothing was copied or bundled.

   RF Atlas instead implements an original bounded FM RDS subset with discriminator/multiplex recovery, 57 kHz demodulation, differential Manchester decoding, CRC-10 polynomial 0x5B9 and A/B/C-or-C-prime/D offsets. Two adjacent complete groups with repeated PI are mandatory. No error correction is attempted. PS requires all four printable segments consistently repeated twice; radio text remains unsupported. Synthetic FM IQ recovery, damaged CRC/ordering rejection and 30 independent noise captures passed. The live Smart campaign verified three captures; offline replay recovered 18, 22 and 22 valid groups with the same PI. Isolated private identifier-retention validation created one RDS broadcast-system identifier with 62 group observations. PS was not verified live. Application retention defaults remain off, and no raw-frame persistence was enabled. PI does not establish a unique physical transmitter or a geographically unique station.

   [NRSC-5](https://github.com/theori-io/nrsc5) supports Ubuntu source builds and Windows MSYS2/cross-builds, offline file input, and C/Python metadata APIs. Its [license](https://github.com/theori-io/nrsc5/blob/master/LICENSE) is GPL-3.0-or-later. The [input API](https://github.com/theori-io/nrsc5/blob/master/include/nrsc5.h) specifies CU8 at 1,488,375 SPS, or native FM CS16/CF32 at 744,187.5 SPS; the README rounds the latter. Current RF Atlas capture rates require conversion. Integration is deferred: a compatible binary/library is absent, fixed-rate conversion and structured evidence need an audited adapter, and Blog V4L compatibility depends on an unvalidated linked librtlsdr build. No HD Radio decode is claimed. No drivers, obsolete GNU Radio environment or external DLLs were installed.

9. **Protocol identification.** Native ADS-B DF17, AX.25 AFSK1200 UI/F0 and RDS are available; separately installed optional rtl_433 WH2/WH5 remain supported. Existing independent valid/corrupt/noise fixtures passed. Live verification in this campaign was RDS only; no live aircraft, sensor or packet decode is claimed. Frequency/rate/capability gates prevent incompatible decoder launches; modulation and bandwidth provide ordering hints rather than proof. Every attempt and compatibility decision remains in identification history. Fresh-visit enqueueing prevents old unrelated catalog channels from consuming the new discovery queue. Age increases job priority, repeat investigations have cooldowns, and Smart exploration gives the least-visited region every third due choice. Failed acquisition retries back off from 60 seconds to at most one hour, resetting the consecutive-failure count after success. Queues, samples, recordings, decoder output and execution time remain bounded.

10. **Inventory and interface.** The existing Discovery Console layout is preserved. Signal details now expose original observation timestamps, their UTC basis, spectral fingerprints, comparable historical observations and frequency spread. IQ characterization, modulation hypotheses, decoder attempts and verified associations remain separate. UTC-only filtering/sorting avoids treating ambiguous history as certain; explicit values render locally in local-time controls and as UTC in UTC-labelled discovery tables. Browser automation verified real catalog counts, visible fingerprint details, no page errors, and actual tuner acquisition/start/stop returning idle. Observations, channel records and retained identifiers have distinct counts; no new physical-emitter count is asserted.

11. **Timestamp migration.** Schema 8 is additive and creates a pre-migration SQLite backup. The existing catalog's 1,457 original observation records were compared with the backup and retained exactly, including original timestamp strings and raw references. Their timezone remains LEGACY_TIMEZONE_UNKNOWN. Forty-four new application observations have receiver-local originals plus explicit UTC and conversion basis; catalog integrity is `ok`. Offset-bearing legacy values normalize safely; naive legacy values stay excluded from UTC windows/timelines and project as NULL in detached analytics. New raw-spectrum time mappings also cover rows with no detections, so historical plots do not need to guess a timezone. Receiver clocks are checked against acquisition-start metadata; long-range or ambiguous historical reinterpretation is not performed. The two baseline audits from this same known host/session were explicitly annotated when converting their audit-only timestamps; this did not reinterpret older project history.

13. **Ubuntu tests.** Actual Ubuntu 24.04.4 with Python 3.12.3: 205 public tests ran in 40.99 seconds, 202 passed and three skipped. 14. **Hardware duration and Full subset.** The longest continuous worker campaign lasted 232.19 seconds (about 3.87 minutes), within a requested 300-second bound with the existing 65-second operation reserve. This is a bounded smoke/soak campaign, not evidence of prolonged stability. Quick controller validation separately lasted 66.40 seconds. A representative 6 MHz Full Spectrum subset completed three 2 MHz segments in 15.05 seconds, with an intentional interruption before segment two, preservation of the first checkpoint/raw reference, and resume of the same run. This was not a full receiver-range sweep. Integrity passed and logical database growth was zero for the no-detection subset. Real IQ remains private under finite recording budgets; acquisition is receive-only.

Original additions are MIT; NumPy/SciPy remain the core dependencies, DuckDB/plots remain optional, and existing uPlot notices are preserved. 16. **Known limitations.** RF sensitivity, gain calibration, receiver settling, edge losses and burst miss probability have not been measured against calibrated injected RF. AM tool output remains invalid in the tested configuration. Broad candidates and repeated channel fragments can still reflect interference or multiple spectral structures from one system. Shape controls do not estimate real-world identity false matches. RDS has limited clock recovery, no correction and no radio text; PS needs more consistent groups than these live captures supplied. The optional WH5 test showed one unresolved intermittent failure. ML remains synthetic-only and worse than DSP here. No HD Radio integration, full-spectrum completion, long-duration reliability or Ubuntu 26.04 support is claimed.

17. **Next priorities.** Measure matched hardware settings with calibrated weak/narrow/burst inputs and repeat sessions; isolate the AM non-finite-output cause without changing drivers; reduce process/retuning overhead while preserving coverage validation; measure field candidate false alarms and improve broad-channel grouping; extend bounded RDS capture/clock recovery and PS validation; investigate the intermittent external WH5 fixture; audit optional NRSC-5 rate conversion/library isolation; and validate Ubuntu 26.04 when available. Keep DSP, conservative UNKNOWN outcomes and explicit evidence provenance as release gates.
